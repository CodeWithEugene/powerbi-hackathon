# Angani Air — Presentation Script (10 minutes, 3 presenters)

Report: **Angani Air Loyalty Decision Brief** (5 pages, Power BI). Target time **8:00**, leaving 2:00 for questions.

Roles:
- **Presenter A (Lead)**: open, Page 1, Page 2. About 3:15.
- **Presenter B**: Page 3, Page 4. About 2:45.
- **Presenter C**: Page 5, close, first answer in Q&A. About 2:00.
- One person clicks the pages (B is a good choice). Speaker never turns to the screen; point with a sentence, not a finger.

Rule for every page: say the **headline sentence first**, then one or two proofs, then hand over. Never read a chart title aloud. Never say "as you can see".

## Page 1 — Executive Answer

**What is on it**
- Header: *The 2018 promotion built our best flyers. Then we lost 12% of them in one quarter.*
- Six KPI cards with a navy accent bar: promo share of growth 72%, standard flights per member 8.4, promo flights per member 46.2, flights YoY +27.9%, promo churn 11.8%, standard churn 2.2%.
- Flights per member by cohort (2018), monthly flights Jan 2017 to Dec 2018, churn by cohort, redemption rate by cohort, a cohort slicer.
- Footnote: source and reconciliation.

**Why it exists**
Judges score "Business Understanding" and "Storytelling". This page answers the management question in one sentence before any chart is explained. Everything else is proof.

**Script — Presenter A (1:15)**

> Good afternoon. In 2018 Angani Air flew sixty-two thousand more flights than the year before. That is a twenty-eight percent increase. *(pause)* Seventy-two percent of that increase came from nine hundred and seventy-one people. Six percent of the programme.
>
> They were recruited in one promotion, February to April 2018. And by December, one in eight of them was gone.
>
> So the question the board asked us was: did the promotion create valuable behaviour? Our answer is yes, spectacularly, and then we lost it. This page is the whole story in six numbers. Promo members fly forty-six times a year. A standard joiner from the same year flies eight. That is five and a half times more. But promo churn is eleven point eight percent against two point two. Five times higher.
>
> The next two pages show why, and the last page shows what to do about it.

Handover line: "Let me show you exactly when it broke."

## Page 2 — Campaign Effectiveness

**What is on it**
- Header: *Campaign Effectiveness: the promotion built our heaviest flyers, then the bonus ended.*
- Line chart: flights per member per month, Jan 2017 to Dec 2018, one line per cohort. The promo line climbs to 8.7 in July 2018 and collapses to 2.8 in September.
- Bar: earn rate, points per km by cohort: promo 1.5, everyone else 1.0.
- Column chart: cancellations by month and cohort, 2013 to 2018. Scroll to the right end: the promo bars appear only in October, November and December.

**Why it exists**
This is the like-for-like comparison the brief demands: promotion versus standard 2018 joiners, not versus the legacy base. It shows the mechanic (a 50% points bonus) and the cliff (engagement drop, then cancellations).

**Script — Presenter A (2:00)**

> This is the promo cohort month by month. May, six flights per member. June, eight. July, eight point seven. August, seven point seven. Then September: two point eight. A sixty-eight percent drop in a single month.
>
> Why? Look at the earn rate. Promo members earned one and a half points per kilometre. Everyone else earned one. A fifty percent bonus. Our reading of the data is that the bonus was time-boxed, roughly six months from joining, and it ran out around September.
>
> Now watch what happens next. *(point to the cancellations chart)* Promo cancellations in August: four. October: thirty. November: thirty-one. December: forty-three. One hundred and four cancellations in one quarter, at a median tenure of exactly eight months.
>
> And here is the part that should worry you. The people who left were not low-value. They flew forty flights each in 2018. We recruited our best customers, gave them a reason to fly, and then took the reason away.

Handover line: "So who should we have been protecting? Presenter B, over to you, because the answer is not who you think."

## Page 3 — Who Is Valuable

**What is on it**
- Header: *Who is valuable? Not a demographic. Behaviour predicts value; segments do not.*
- Three flat bar charts: flights per member by loyalty card, by province, by education. Every bar sits at about 30.
- Column chart: churn % by "Ever Redeemed" and cohort. Never-redeemed legacy members churn at over 40%; redeemers at about 5%.

**Why it exists**
This is the non-obvious insight judges reward. Most teams will slice by demographics and report small differences. We show the slices are flat and pivot to the real driver.

**Script — Presenter B (1:30)**

> The natural instinct is to target a segment. Gold card holders. Ontario. Graduates. We tested all of them, and here is the uncomfortable truth: they are identical.
>
> Aurora members fly thirty point six times over two years. Nova, thirty point five. Star, thirty point three. Every province, every education level, every marital status lands between twenty-nine and thirty-three. The lifetime value field in the data has a correlation of minus zero point zero zero five with actual flying. Zero. If you allocate loyalty budget by demographic, you are guessing.
>
> Now look at the one split that does matter. Members who have redeemed points at least once churn at four point nine percent. Members who never have churn at forty point eight percent. Eight times higher. And only thirty-five percent of standard 2018 joiners have ever redeemed. Redemption is not a cost. It is the retention lever.

Handover line: "And that leads to the biggest number in this whole analysis."

## Page 4 — Points Economics

**What is on it**
- Header: *Points economics: 98.5% of points are never redeemed. Promo members are banking CDN 18.7k each.*
- Four KPI cards: points never redeemed 98.5%, unredeemed liability CDN 141M, promo liability per member CDN 19K, promo redemption rate 0.37%.
- Bar: unredeemed value per member by cohort, 2018.
- Column chart: points earned versus points redeemed by cohort, 2018. The redeemed bars are almost invisible next to the earned bars.

**Why it exists**
It converts a behavioural finding into money. Executives act on balance-sheet numbers. It also explains why the promo cohort is fragile: they hold a lot of a currency they never use.

**Script — Presenter B (1:15)**

> Ninety-eight point five percent of all points earned in this programme are never redeemed. Seven hundred and eighty-four million points. At the observed redemption value of eighteen cents a point, that is roughly one hundred and forty-one million Canadian dollars.
>
> That is either a liability sitting on your balance sheet, or proof that members do not value the currency. Either way, it is a problem you can fix.
>
> The promo cohort is the extreme case. They earned points fifty percent faster and redeemed zero point three seven percent of them. Each promo member is holding about eighteen thousand seven hundred dollars of unredeemed value. A standard joiner holds under three thousand. We handed them a wallet and never showed them the shop.

Handover line: "So what does management do on Monday morning? Presenter C."

## Page 5 — Decisions

**What is on it**
- Header: *Decisions: who to target, what to offer, where to invest.* Subtitle: *Run the promotion again. It works. But taper the bonus instead of ending it, make every member redeem once in the first 90 days, and win back the 115 high-flyers who left.*
- Four rows of cards on a grey canvas:
  - **Who to target**: retained promotion members · 2018 joiners who never redeemed · stop demographic targeting.
  - **What to offer**: first redemption inside 90 days · re-run the promotion with a taper · win back the 115 who left. Each card names an owner and an expected effect.
  - **Where to invest**: months 6 to 9 of the lifecycle · redemption incentives, not regions · fix the points balance.
  - **Method**: clean data · like-for-like baseline · reconciled to source.

**Why it exists**
"Decision-Focused Insights" is scored on whether the viewer knows what to do next. Every card is an action with an owner and an effect, not an observation.

**Script — Presenter C (1:45)**

> Three decisions.
>
> **Who.** The eight hundred and fifty-six promotion members who are still with us, flying forty-six times a year each. And every 2018 joiner who has never redeemed a point, sixty-five percent of them. Not a province. Not a card tier. Behaviour.
>
> **What.** Three offers. One: a first redemption inside ninety days, owned by Loyalty Marketing. It moves new members from the forty-one percent churn group to the five percent group. Two: run the promotion again, but taper the bonus. One point five, then one point two five, then one, across months six to twelve. No cliff. Owned by Loyalty Product. Halving promo churn keeps about two and a half thousand flights a year for every thousand recruited. Three: a win-back offer to the one hundred and fifteen who left. They flew forty flights each. Owned by CRM.
>
> **Where.** Move budget out of segment acquisition and into months six to nine of the member lifecycle, and into redemption. Spend flat across provinces, because none of them outperforms. And decide what to do about one hundred and forty-one million dollars of unused points.
>
> One line on method, so you can trust every number here. We removed one thousand nine hundred and twenty-two duplicate rows, compared the promotion with 2018 standard joiners rather than the legacy base, and every figure reconciles to source: five hundred and eight thousand flights, sixteen thousand seven hundred members.

**Close — Presenter C (0:20)**

> We started with sixty-two thousand extra flights and nine hundred and seventy-one people. Here is the ending. The promotion is the best thing this programme has done. Run it again. Just do not let the bonus end on a cliff, make every new member redeem once, and go and get the one hundred and fifteen back. Thank you.

## Transitions and stagecraft

- Hand over with the last sentence of your section, not with "I'll now hand over to". The handover lines are written above.
- Each presenter says their own name once, at their first line, nothing more.
- Pause for one breath after every big number. Let "seventy-two percent" land before the next sentence.
- Clicker (B) changes page on the handover line, not before.
- If the slicer or a chart is clicked by accident, press the page tab again to reset.
- Do not scroll the cancellations chart during the talk; have it pre-scrolled to the right end (2018) before you start.

## Likely questions, one-line answers (anyone can take these)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Forty-six flights a year is unrealistic. | It is the whole cohort. Median forty-four, interquartile range thirty-two to fifty-eight. Ninety-five percent of promo members flew after April. |
| Isn't comparing with legacy fairer? | No. Legacy members have years of tenure. Standard 2018 joiners are the like-for-like group: same year, same programme, different offer. They fly eight. |
| Why not use CLV? | CLV is lifetime invoice history. For someone who joined in March 2018 it cannot describe 2018 behaviour. Its correlation with flying is minus zero point zero zero five. |
| Where is eighteen cents a point from? | Total redemption cost, two point two million dollars, divided by points redeemed, twelve point three million. |
| Is the one hundred and forty-one million a real liability? | It is the unredeemed balance at observed value. Whether it is booked depends on expiry policy. Either it is a cost waiting to happen or a currency nobody values. |
| Does redemption cause retention or just correlate? | Both are true and we say so. But redemption is the lever the airline controls, and a first-redemption nudge is cheap to test. |
| How do you know the bonus ended in September? | Inferred from behaviour: flights per member fall sixty-eight percent in September, cancellations spike in October, median tenure eight months. Management can confirm the terms. |
| What about the missing salaries? | Four thousand two hundred and thirty-eight, all College educated, so a systematic gap. Kept the members, never used salary in a headline number. |
